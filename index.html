Why Do We Treat the Internet Like It Isn't Real?

One of the biggest problems with the internet is how easily we forget that there are real people behind the screen.

A good example of this is the idea of “lolcows”. These are people who become internet jokes because others find their behaviour strange, embarrassing, or entertaining. People watch them, make fun of them, and sometimes spend years creating content about them. Eventually, they stop being seen as people and become characters that others watch for entertainment.

I find this strange because it shows how differently we behave online. I've seen how easily people join in on making fun of someone without thinking much about the person behind the screen. Thousands of people can laugh at one person, even when the joke is about something that could genuinely hurt them.

I think part of the problem is that we often call the world outside the internet “real life”. This makes it seem like the online world is something separate, even though it is such a big part of our daily lives. It's easy to forget that the people we see online have their own lives, feelings, and problems.

I don't think there's anything wrong with laughing at strange situations or criticising someone's behaviour. But I do think there's a difference between making a joke and turning someone into a joke. It's something worth thinking about when we spend so much of our time online.

I hope that, in the future, we become more aware of how we treat people on the internet. After all, the online world isn't as separate from real life as we sometimes like to think.

The Dehumanization of People on the Internet

Overview

The dehumanization of people on the internet is when people are treated as jokes or entertainment instead of human beings.

An example is the term “lolcow”. This describes a person who becomes an internet joke because others find their behaviour strange or embarrassing. People may spend years making fun of them without thinking about how it affects them.

The Problem

The internet can make people forget that there are real people behind the screen. Things that would be considered cruel in real life can become normal entertainment online.

We often call the world outside the internet “real life”, even though the internet is part of our everyday lives.

Conclusion

The internet should be seen as an extension of real life. People should be able to laugh and criticise others without turning them into objects of entertainment. Remembering that there is a real person behind every screen could make the internet a more respectful place.
